# Chapter 7: Workflow - Diátaxis as a Guide to Work

## Core Idea

Diátaxis is a guide, not a plan. It discourages planning and top-down workflows - running counter to much accepted documentation wisdom - in favour of small, responsive iterations from which overall structure emerges by itself.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Guide, not plan**: the structure Diátaxis describes is not something you must complete; it's a map to check you're in the right place, going in the right directions. Its point is to give you a way to think about and understand your documentation - tools to assess it, identify where its problems lie, and judge what to do to improve it.
- **The just-do-something loop**: the prescribed way to apply Diátaxis to any existing documentation, however messy:
  1. **Choose something** - any piece. Don't go looking for outstanding problems; take the file you're in, the last page you read, or literally anything at random.
  2. **Assess it** - preferably something small: a page, or better, a paragraph or sentence. Challenge it against Diátaxis standards: What user need is represented here? How well does it serve that need? What can be added, moved, removed, or changed to serve it better? Do its language and logic meet the requirements of this mode?
  3. **Decide what to do** - what single next action will produce an immediate improvement here?
  4. **Do it** - complete that one action and consider it completed: publish it, or at least commit the change.
  5. Return to the beginning.
- **Well-formed organic growth**: the model for documentation development. Organic growth happens at the cellular level; the organism's structure is guaranteed by the healthy development of cells according to rules appropriate to each kind of cell - never imposed from above or outside. Good structure develops from within.
- **Complete, not finished**: a plant is never finished - it can always develop further - but at every stage from seed to mature tree it is complete: nothing is missing for its stage of development. Documentation likewise is never finished (product and needs keep changing) but can always be complete: useful to users, appropriate to its current stage, structurally healthy, ready for the next stage.

## Key Concepts

- **Structure from the inside**: keep following Diátaxis prompts and your documentation will assume the Diátaxis structure - because it has been improved. Not the other way round, that structure must be imposed to improve it. At a certain point your changes will appear to demand that material move under a Diátaxis heading; that is how the top-level structure forms.
- **Publish every step**: every step in the right direction is worth publishing immediately; resist accumulating large tranches of work to show something substantial.

## Mental Models

- Don't work on the big picture. Diátaxis provides a big picture, but it's designed to guide small steps; keep taking small steps to arrive where you want to go.
- The loop removes the most paralysing part of a documentation writer's work: working out what to do. Work keeps flowing in the right direction without expending energy on a plan.

## Anti-patterns

- **Empty scaffolding**: creating empty tutorials/how-to/reference/explanation sections with nothing in them. "Don't do that. It's horrible."
- **Tear-down-and-restart**: when tidying a huge mess, the temptation is to demolish and begin again. Avoid it; whatever the state of the documentation, it's always possible to improve it iteratively.
- **Batching for substance**: waiting to publish until you have something big to show.
- **Plan-and-execute cycles**: the strong urge to plan toward results has better alternatives when working with documentation.

## Worked Example

A first hour with a messy documentation set, following the loop:

1. Choose: the page open in front of you - an "FAQ" that mixes install steps, option tables, and design rationale.
2. Assess one paragraph: three sentences explaining why the daemon uses a lock file, sitting between two install steps. User need: understanding (explanation), trapped inside action guidance.
3. Decide: move those three sentences to a new "About the lock file" note and leave a link.
4. Do it, commit it, publish it.
5. Back to step 1 - probably the very next paragraph.

No restructure was planned; after enough cycles like this, the material itself starts demanding its Diátaxis headings.

## Key Takeaways

1. Never restructure top-down and never create empty section scaffolding; let structure form from repeated small improvements.
2. Run the loop: choose anything, assess it against the user need it serves, do the one action that improves it, publish, repeat.
3. Prefer the smallest assessable unit - a sentence or paragraph beats a page.
4. Documentation is never finished, but keep it always complete: appropriate and healthy for its current stage.
