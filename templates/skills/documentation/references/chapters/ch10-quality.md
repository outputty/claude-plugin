# Chapter 10: Towards a Theory of Quality in Documentation

## Core Idea

Documentation quality splits into two distinct kinds: functional quality (accuracy, completeness, consistency - objective, measurable, independent properties) and deep quality (flow, fitting human needs, feeling good to use - subjective, interdependent, judged not measured). Diátaxis cannot give you functional quality, but it exposes lapses in it - and it lays down conditions for the possibility of deep quality.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Functional quality**: accuracy, completeness, consistency, usefulness, precision. Independent of each other (documentation can be accurate without being complete; complete but inaccurate; accurate, complete, consistent - and useless). Attaining it means meeting high, objectively-measurable standards in multiple independent dimensions, consistently; any failure is readily apparent to the user.
- **Deep quality**: feeling good to use, having flow, fitting to human needs, being beautiful, anticipating the user. Cannot be checked or measured, but can be clearly identified when encountered.
- **The relationship between them**:

  | Functional quality          | Deep quality                        |
  | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
  | independent characteristics | interdependent characteristics      |
  | objective                   | subjective                          |
  | measured against the world  | assessed against the human          |
  | a condition of deep quality | conditional upon functional quality |
  | aspects of constraint       | aspects of liberation               |

  Deep quality is conditional on functional quality: no user experiences documentation as beautiful if it's inaccurate, or enjoys how it anticipates their needs if it's inconsistent - the moment a lapse appears, the experience is tarnished.

## Key Concepts

- **Measurement vs judgement**: functional aspects can be measured, sometimes literally with numbers (completeness); deep qualities can only be enquired into and interrogated - judgements, not measurements.
- **Constraint vs liberation**: every functional characteristic is a burden - a test you might fail, re-checked at every release. Anticipating needs and flow are the work of creativity and taste: to attain functional quality we conform; to attain deep quality we invent.
- **Flow (in documentation)**: movement from one stage or state to another that seems right, unforced, in sympathy with the concerns of the moment and the way minds and bodies work.

## Mental Models

- The clothing analogy: functional quality is keeping you warm and dry (measurable by anyone); deep quality is how a garment hangs, moves, and was shaped (recognising _what_ makes it excellent takes a trained eye) - yet anyone can feel _that_ it's excellent, because it fits and moves with them. Same with documentation: users may not be able to say why it's good, but it will feel good, or not - and that's what matters. Creators, though, must develop the trained eye: producing deep quality depends on understanding what makes documentation good, not just that it is.
- Diátaxis serves deep quality directly: its categories exist as a response to needs (fitting), and its boundary discipline prevents the rhythm-disruption that kills flow (e.g. a digression into explanation interrupting a how-to guide).

## Anti-patterns

- **Mistaking Diátaxis for a formula**: it offers principles, not a short-cut bypassing UX, interaction design, or visual design. Using it does not guarantee deep quality - it can't be all that is required.
- **Stopping at functional quality**: accurate, consistent, even very useful documentation can still be awkward and unpleasant to use.

## Worked Example

How Diátaxis exposes functional lapses, from the chapter's own cases:

1. Reference architecture is made to mirror the code architecture (a Diátaxis recommendation) - and gaps in the documentation suddenly become clearly visible: the structure has empty slots where the code has occupied ones.
2. Explanatory verbiage is moved out of a tutorial (per Diátaxis demands) - and a section stands exposed where the reader had been left to work something out for themselves; the explanation had been papering over a missing step.

In both cases Diátaxis played only an analytical role for functional quality - it revealed the problem; fixing accuracy or completeness is still the writer's craft.

## Key Takeaways

1. Meet functional quality first: deep quality is impossible without it, and every lapse is visible to users.
2. Judge deep quality against the human, not the world: does it fit, flow, anticipate?
3. Use Diátaxis restructuring as a diagnostic: moving content to its right quadrant makes hidden gaps and hand-waving visible.
4. Expect the functional characteristics to feel like constraints and the deep ones to feel like invention - both are the job.
