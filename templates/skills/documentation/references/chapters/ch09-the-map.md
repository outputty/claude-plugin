# Chapter 9: The Map

## Core Idea

Diátaxis works as an organising scheme because it is a two-dimensional structure, not a list: each form occupies a space in relationship to its neighbours, and the boundaries between them highlight their distinctions - and mark exactly where documentation goes wrong.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Expectations and guidance**: organising material by the map gives readers clear expectations and authors clear guidance - what any piece of content is for, how it should be written, where it should be placed:

  |  | Tutorials | How-to guides | Reference | Explanation |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | what they do | introduce, educate, lead | guide | state, describe, inform | explain, clarify, discuss |
  | answers the question | "Can you teach me to…?" | "How do I…?" | "What is…?" | "Why…?" |
  | oriented to | learning | goals | information | understanding |
  | purpose | to provide a learning experience | to help achieve a particular goal | to describe the machinery | to illuminate a topic |
  | form | a lesson | a series of steps | dry description | discursive explanation |
  | analogy | teaching a child how to cook | a recipe in a cookery book | information on the back of a food packet | an article on culinary social history |

- **Blur**: each form has a natural affinity with its neighbours on the map, and a natural tendency to blur into them:
  - tutorials and how-to guides both _guide action_
  - how-to guides and reference both _serve the application of skill_
  - reference and explanation both _contain propositional knowledge_
  - explanation and tutorials both _serve the acquisition of skill_

  When distinctions blur, kinds bleed into each other, writing style and content land in inappropriate places, and structural problems follow - making disciplined writing still harder. Worst case: complete or partial collapse of tutorials and how-to guides into each other, making it impossible to meet the needs served by either.

- **The journey around the map**: a cycle of documentation needs corresponding to how people actually become expert in a craft:
  1. learning-oriented phase: we begin by learning - diving straight in to do it, under a teacher's guidance if we're lucky
  2. goal-oriented phase: we put the skill to work
  3. information-oriented phase: work calls on knowledge not in our head, so we consult reference
  4. explanation-oriented phase: away from the work, we reflect on practice and knowledge to understand the whole
  - Then back to the beginning, for a new thing to grasp or to penetrate deeper. Don't read it too literally: a real user enters anywhere and moves between kinds moment to moment - but the cycle's ordering has sense and meaning.

## Key Concepts

- **The problem of structure**: absent a clear, generalised documentation architecture, creators structure work around product features - rarely successful even once, and wildly inconsistent across a portfolio.
- **Architectural faults infect content**: bad structure isn't only a navigation problem; it undermines what gets written.
- **Any orderly scheme helps**: even an imperfect content-category scheme improves documentation for authors and users by providing lists of content types - but list-schemes feel arbitrary (why this list? which competing list?), which is what the two-dimensional map answers.

## Mental Models

- Use the six-row table as a lint pass: pick any page, read the column it claims to be, and check each row - the question it answers, its orientation, its form. A row mismatch tells you which neighbour it's blurring into.
- Blur travels along edges, not diagonals: a tutorial decays into a how-to guide or an explanation, rarely into reference. Knowing the shared edge tells you what leaked (action guidance, propositional knowledge, study material, work material).

## Anti-patterns

- **Structuring documentation around product features**: the default failure mode absent a scheme.
- **Letting neighbours merge**: especially tutorials and how-to guides - the collapse that defeats both needs at once.

## Key Takeaways

1. Diátaxis structures documentation as a map with meaningful boundaries, not a list of section names - that's why it doesn't feel arbitrary.
2. Memorise the four questions: "Can you teach me to…?" / "How do I…?" / "What is…?" / "Why…?" - they classify content fastest.
3. Watch the four shared edges; every blur is one of the four affinities, and naming which edge tells you the fix.
4. Users cycle through learning, goals, information, understanding as they grow expert - serve the cycle, but don't force reading order.
