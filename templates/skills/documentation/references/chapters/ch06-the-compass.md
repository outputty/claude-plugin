# Chapter 6: The Compass

## Core Idea

The Diátaxis compass is a truth-table of documentation: two questions - action or cognition? acquisition or application? - that classify any piece of content or any user need into one of the four forms. It is the course-correction tool for when intuition fails or, worse, answers immediately and wrongly.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The Diátaxis compass**: reduces a two-dimensional problem to its simpler parts.

  | If the content…   | …and serves the user's… | …then it must belong to… |
  | ----------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------ |
  | informs action    | acquisition of skill    | a tutorial               |
  | informs action    | application of skill    | a how-to guide           |
  | informs cognition | application of skill    | reference                |
  | informs cognition | acquisition of skill    | explanation              |
  - When to use: whenever you face "what form of documentation is this?" or "what form is needed here?" with no obvious answer - or when you think you're doing one thing but are troubled by doubt or difficulty in the work. It applies equally to user situations that need documentation and to existing documentation that needs to be moved or improved.
  - How: ask the two questions; the table yields the answer. "Like many good tools, it's surprisingly banal."

- **Flexible terms**: especially when finding initial bearings, don't get fixated on the exact names:
  - action: practical steps, doing
  - cognition: theoretical or propositional knowledge, thinking
  - acquisition: study
  - application: work

## Key Concepts

- **A map needs a compass**: the map is a reminder of the kinds and their relationships and accords with intuition - but intuition is not always reliable, and a map is most powerful in unfamiliar territory when you also have a compass.
- **Forced reconsideration**: the compass's value is that it makes you stop and reconsider when doubt appears mid-work.

## Mental Models

- Rephrase the two questions to fit the moment:
  - Do I think I am writing for x or y?
  - Is this writing in front of me engaged in x or y?
  - Does the user need x or y?
  - Do I want to x or y?
- Apply the questions at any zoom level: close-up, at the level of sentences and words, or from a wider perspective over an entire document.

## Anti-patterns

- **Trusting the immediate intuitive answer**: sometimes intuition provides an immediate answer that is also wrong; the compass exists precisely for that case.
- **Fixating on the exact terms**: the compass's words are handles, not tests to pass; use the flexible synonyms.

## Worked Example

A doubt resolved with the two questions: you're writing a page titled "Configuration options", and it's drifting - you keep adding advice about when to choose each option.

1. Does the option list inform action or cognition? Cognition - it's propositional knowledge about the machinery.
2. Does it serve acquisition or application? Application - the user consults it while working.
3. Verdict: reference. The "when to choose" advice informs cognition for acquisition - that's explanation; move it to its own topic page and link.

One page, two questions per fragment, and the mixed content separates cleanly.

## Key Takeaways

1. Two questions classify anything: action or cognition, acquisition or application.
2. Use the compass on documentation you're writing, documentation you're assessing, and user situations that need documentation.
3. Apply it at sentence level and at document level - blur happens at both.
4. Reach for it exactly when you feel doubt or difficulty: that friction is the signal that you're probably in the wrong quadrant.
