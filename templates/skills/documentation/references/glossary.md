# Glossary

**Acquisition** - the "at study" pole of the acquisition/application dimension: the user acquiring the skill and knowledge of their craft. Served by tutorials and explanation. (Ch 6, Ch 8)

**Action** - the practical half of the action/cognition dimension: practical knowledge, knowing how, what we do. Tutorials and how-to guides inform action. (Ch 6, Ch 8)

**Application** - the "at work" pole of the acquisition/application dimension: the user applying the skill and knowledge of their craft. Served by how-to guides and reference. (Ch 6, Ch 8)

**Blur** - the tendency of each documentation form to bleed into its neighbours on the map along their shared edges; the root of a vast number of documentation problems. (Ch 9)

**Cognition** - the theoretical half of the action/cognition dimension: propositional knowledge, knowing that, what we think. Reference and explanation inform cognition. (Ch 6, Ch 8)

**Compass (the Diátaxis compass)** - the decision tool: content that informs action or cognition, serving acquisition or application, must belong to a tutorial, how-to guide, reference, or explanation respectively. (Ch 6)

**Complete, not finished** - the state good documentation is always in: never finished (it must keep adapting) but always complete - useful, appropriate to its stage of development, structurally healthy. (Ch 7)

**Conditional imperative** - the characteristic sentence form of how-to guides: "If you want x, do y. To achieve w, do z." (Ch 3)

**Craft** - what defines a domain of skill; using a tool or product is a craft, as is a discipline or profession. The practitioner of a craft is the user Diátaxis serves. (Ch 8)

**Deep quality** - the subjective, interdependent qualities of documentation: feeling good to use, having flow, fitting human needs, being beautiful, anticipating the user. Judged, not measured; conditional on functional quality. (Ch 10)

**Diátaxis** - from Ancient Greek dia ("across") and taxis ("arrangement"): a systematic approach to technical documentation authoring built on four kinds of documentation serving four user needs. (Ch 1)

**Explanation** - understanding-oriented documentation: discursive treatment of a topic that permits reflection; brings clarity, light, and context; answers "Why…?". (Ch 5)

**Feeling of doing** - the accomplished practitioner's joined-up purpose, action, thinking, and result; the experience a tutorial's tasks should cradle. (Ch 2)

**Flow** - movement from one state to another that seems right, unforced, in sympathy with the reader's concerns and the way minds and bodies work; a deep-quality characteristic and a how-to guide imperative. (Ch 3, Ch 10)

**Functional quality** - the objective, independent, measurable qualities of documentation: accuracy, completeness, consistency, usefulness, precision. A condition of deep quality. (Ch 10)

**How-to guide** - goal-oriented documentation: directions that guide an already-competent user through a real-world problem or toward a result; answers "How do I…?". (Ch 3)

**Journey around the map** - the cycle of documentation needs mirroring how people become expert: learning, then goals, then information, then understanding, and around again. (Ch 9)

**Map (the Diátaxis map)** - the two-dimensional arrangement of the four documentation forms, defined by action/cognition and acquisition/application; a complete map with necessarily four quarters. (Ch 8, Ch 9)

**Narrative of the expected** - a tutorial's running feedback confirming the learner is on the right path: "You will notice that…", "The output should look something like…". (Ch 2)

**Reference** - information-oriented documentation: austere, authoritative technical description of the machinery, structured to mirror it; answers "What is…?". (Ch 4)

**Tutorial** - learning-oriented documentation: a lesson; a practical learning experience under a tutor's guidance, whose obligation is the learner's success; answers "Can you teach me to…?". (Ch 2)

**Well-formed organic growth** - the model for documentation development: structure guaranteed by the healthy development of individual cells according to their own rules, never imposed from outside. (Ch 7)

**Work/study test** - the discriminator for both hard boundaries: is this needed while working at the task, or once the reader steps away to think? Work means how-to guide or reference; study means tutorial or explanation. (Ch 11, Ch 12)
